## New major comments

1. **Both Appendix-B viscous-unit conversions are a factor of 10 too
   large.** The text quotes "0.01c, i.e. y⁺≈420 in the viscous units of
   the reattached wall, u_τ taken at x=0.70" and "d=0.0014c
   (y⁺≈58)". Measured on the case: Cf(0.70, upper) = 8.9×10⁻⁴ →
   u_τ = √(Cf/2)·U∞ = 2.11×10⁻³ (a∞ units, U∞ = M = 0.1) → with
   ν = 5×10⁻⁷: **y⁺(0.01c) ≈ 42** and **y⁺(0.0014c) ≈ 5.9**.
   Independent cross-check from the solver's own `yPlus` array
   (0.0070 at the first cell, h0 = 1.7×10⁻⁶c per tab:eppmesh):
   u_τ/ν = 4.1×10³/c → y⁺(0.01c) = 41, y⁺(0.0014c) = 5.7. The quoted
   420/58 are exactly what one gets by taking u_τ = √(Cf/2) *without*
   the ×U∞ = 0.1 — a Mach-normalization slip. Note the correction
   changes the physical reading: peak 2 sits at y⁺ ≈ 6 (sublayer/lower
   buffer), not y⁺ ≈ 58 (log layer) — if anything a *better* fit to
   "new near-wall reattachment layer". Fix both numbers (and keep the
   d/c values, which are correct).

2. **The notch mechanism claim is contradicted by the committed
   diagnostic itself: the row-2 notch is a probe-window truncation, not
   a physical collapse.** Appendix B: "Between x≈0.51 and 0.62 the
   trace collapses to ≈0.14: the handover … diffuses exactly the
   inflection and concentrated shear that carry the outer branch — a
   deeper probe shows its residue dying by x≈0.55 — … the wall-normal
   max of one decaying and one growing branch necessarily notches at
   their crossing." Three problems, all measured on the same solution:
   (a) *the outer branch does not die by 0.55* — probing the committed
   P field to 0.025c, the 0.01–0.025c band max is 1.02/1.05/1.08/1.10/
   1.07 at x = 0.498/0.512/0.530/0.549/0.571 and stays >0.3 until
   x ≈ 0.69: the lifted layer is fully saturated just above the row-2
   window throughout the "collapse"; (b) *the full envelope has no
   notch* — max-P over d≤0.025c has minimum 0.85 at x = 0.615 (the
   committed `diag_sg_doublepeak.py`'s own middle panel, window 0.012c,
   already shows this: no dip anywhere near 0.14); (c) even within the
   committed 0.01c trace the collapse-to-0.14 spans 0.507–0.53 only —
   P is back to 0.47 by 0.549 and 0.85 by 0.615, so "between 0.51 and
   0.62" misdescribes the curve the reader will look at. What actually
   happens: the lifted shear layer *climbs out of the fixed 0.01c
   window* (the text's own "has climbed to the top of the row-2 probe
   window" is the clue), the argmax snaps to the still-weak near-wall
   region, and the trace re-rises as the descending/reattaching layer
   re-enters the window. The χ-crossing alignment is real but
   correlational (the layer lifts because the bubble grows toward
   reattachment; the handover completes there too). Recommend: rewrite
   the notch sentence as what it is — the rate-carrying layer leaving
   the fixed probe window, with the handover crossings bracketing it —
   and drop "diffuses exactly the inflection…" and "necessarily notches
   at their crossing" (or re-support them with a window-following
   probe, which the committed data does not). Stating the row-2 window
   depth (0.01c) in the five-row conventions paragraph — it is
   currently stated nowhere except this Appendix-B parenthesis — would
   make the window-artifact reading available to the reader. The
   two-peak *assignments* (lifted layer at the window top; near-wall
   reattachment layer at 0.0014c) survive and are verified.
