## New major comments

1. **The chartreuse participant-m curve is painted B-C green in both
   new figures — the community's most extreme outlier is visually
   attributed to the Bas–Cakmakcioglu submittal.** `ws_style` in
   `regen_nlf_aft_comparison.py` (and the copy in
   `regen_nlf_polar.py`) matches `'algebraic' in fam.lower()`, and
   `m_chart`'s family string — "unknown (chartreuse; … bypass-like/
   **algebraic** behavior)" — matches it, so both `l` (the actual
   B-C submittal) and `m_chart` (attribution unknown; not in the
   Case-3 legend palette) draw in seagreen at highlight weight.
   Pixel check of the rendered PDFs confirms two seagreen polylines
   per panel, and the *conspicuous* green curve in each is m_chart:
   the near-leading-edge front at x_t/c ≈ 0.02–0.12 spanning the
   whole lift axis in fig:nlfaft, and the far-right high-drag polar
   (c_d 0.010→0.0154) in fig:nlfpolar — while the real B-C curve
   hides inside the pack (c_d min 0.0053, top 0.0101). The captions
   say "the algebraic Bas–Cakmakcioglu submittal green" (singular),
   so a reader charges B-C with a curve the deck cannot attribute to
   any model. Fix: match the family key explicitly (e.g.
   `fam.startswith('algebraic/BC')` or key `== 'l'`) in both scripts
   and regenerate both figures; m_chart falls back to light gray,
   which is what the JSON's "unknown" warrants.

2. **"The one published SA-based transition-model result on this
   benchmark" (the restored γ-SA sentence, renders p. 30) is
   contradicted by the paper's own fig:epppolar two paragraphs
   earlier.** SA-BC — plotted on that figure from
   Ref. [shahjahan_2024] and called "the algebraic SA-BCM model"
   elsewhere in the paper (l. 2695) — is an SA-based transition
   model on exactly this benchmark, and the same fix round just
   restored a caveat about it. What the sentence means is the one
   published SA-based *transported-intermittency* (γ-equation)
   result. One-word-class fix: "The one published SA-based
   transported-intermittency result on this benchmark…" (or "the
   only γ-equation coupling to SA published on this benchmark").
